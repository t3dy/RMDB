## Definition and Foundational Claims

Astrology—the practice of understanding how celestial bodies influence terrestrial events and human affairs—was foundational to Renaissance magic. Unlike modern astrology (personal horoscopes), Renaissance astrology was a comprehensive natural philosophy claiming that celestial influences operated through lawful, natural causation.

The celestial realm was understood as perfect, eternal, unchanging. The terrestrial realm was imperfect, transient, changeable. Yet the two were connected through hidden influences: the planets governed terrestrial affairs through natural causation. By understanding these influences, one could predict events and align actions with propitious celestial moments.

## Structure of Astrological Influence

Renaissance astrology integrated Ptolemaic cosmology, Neoplatonic correspondence, medical theory (humoral balances), and magical practice. Each planet governed specific human qualities, bodily organs, herbs, stones, metals. By understanding these correspondences, a physician could prescribe remedies aligned with planetary influence. A magician could create talismans at auspicious celestial moments. A politician could time important decisions to favorable planetary hours.

**The Planets and Their Governance:**
- **Sun:** Heart, intellect, authority, illumination
- **Moon:** Brain, imagination, receptivity, emotional nature
- **Mars:** Muscles, aggression, courage, conflict
- **Venus:** Kidneys, love, pleasure, beauty
- **Mercury:** Lungs, intellect, communication, trade
- **Jupiter:** Expansion, wisdom, beneficence, good fortune
- **Saturn:** Limitation, melancholy, time, discipline

## Astrological Methods and Practice

Renaissance astrology employed several techniques:

**Natal Astrology:** Casting a horoscope based on the exact moment of birth, showing the positions and aspects of celestial bodies at that moment. This horoscope was believed to determine fundamental character and life trajectory.

**Electional Astrology:** Choosing auspicious times for important actions (starting a business, beginning a journey, conducting magical operations) by calculating when celestial configuration would be favorable for the desired outcome.

**Horary Astrology:** Answering specific questions through analysis of the celestial configuration at the moment the question was asked.

**Astrological Medicine:** Prescribing treatments at times when celestial influence supported healing. For example, bleeding or administering purgatives at times when planets were in positions supporting purification and detoxification.

**Astrological Magic:** Creating talismans and performing magical operations at times when celestial configuration would support the desired magical effect. A talisman made during the hour of Venus would carry Venus's influence for matters of love.

## Renaissance Development and Transmission

Renaissance astrology inherited from medieval Islamic and European sources sophisticated accounts of how celestial influences operated. Islamic astronomers and philosophers had developed precise mathematical techniques for calculating planetary positions and aspects. These techniques were transmitted to Renaissance Europe through Latin translations of Islamic texts and through the work of European astronomers building on Islamic foundations.

Key Renaissance astrological figures included Ficino (integrating astrology with Neoplatonic medicine), Agrippa (systematizing astrological correspondences), and Dee (using mathematical astronomy to ground astrological operations).

## The Copernican Challenge

Copernican heliocentrism fundamentally challenged astrological theory. If the Earth is not the center of the cosmos, what justifies the notion that celestial bodies uniquely influence terrestrial affairs? Yet astrology persisted and adapted. Renaissance thinkers developed new theoretical justifications. Kepler, despite heliocentrism, defended celestial influence through new theoretical frameworks (magnetic influence rather than emanation).

Modern science discarded astrological theory entirely. Yet the intuition underlying astrology—that celestial bodies and terrestrial events are connected—persists in contemporary physics (gravity as non-local influence) and in modern statistical correlations (seasonal effects on human psychology, for example).

## Historiographic Status

Astrology exemplifies the coherence of Renaissance natural philosophy. Within the framework of Neoplatonic emanationism and astrological correspondence, claiming that celestial bodies influenced terrestrial events was not superstition but a rational expression of natural philosophical principles. Timing important actions according to celestial configuration was not magical superstition but the application of natural law.

Yet by the 17th century, mechanical philosophy made astrological influence seem incoherent. How could celestial bodies influence terrestrial things without mechanical contact? The concept became untenable within the new physics.

Contemporary scholarship recognizes astrology as a serious natural philosophy within Renaissance frameworks, even while acknowledging that astrological theory is scientifically false by modern standards.

## Key Figures

**Ficino:** Integrating astrology with medicine and Neoplatonic correspondence.  
**Agrippa:** Systematizing astrological correspondences in *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*.  
**Dee:** Using mathematics to ground astrological practice.  
**Kepler:** Defending astrology through new theoretical frameworks after heliocentrism.

## Conclusion

Astrology was not peripheral to Renaissance natural philosophy but foundational. Understanding Renaissance magic requires understanding that astrology was understood as legitimate natural philosophy, grounded in the belief that celestial bodies operated on terrestrial affairs through lawful natural causation. The decline of astrology reflects not the irrationality of Renaissance thinkers but the triumph of mechanical philosophy and the abandonment of Neoplatonic emanationism as explanatory framework.
